How to use Tape Board

Created by R. Heller

Design a circuit board for 3D printing. You print the plate, press copper tape onto the raised lines, and solder component legs through the holes.

Quick start

  1. Choose a part such as Resistor from the Parts list (left), then click the board to place it. Press Confirm (or Enter) to keep it.
  2. Choose the Tape tool. Click where tape starts, then click where it ends. Press Confirm (Enter) to keep that piece, or Extend (E) to keep it and carry on from where it ended. Press Esc to end the run.
  3. Press Export STL and print the file.

Tools (top bar)

  • Tape (T): draw copper tape runs. Part (P): place the selected part. Select (S): click a part to pick it and give it its own hole and pad size (see below). Move (V): drag a part, a tape run or a hole. Rotate: click a part to turn it 90°. Hole (H): add a mounting screw hole. Erase (X): remove a part, hole or tape run.

Confirming changes

With Confirm changes ticked (top bar), every change waits for approval. While it waits you can drag it to a better spot. For tape, drag the green corner dots to reshape, or drag the piece to move it. Press Enter to keep or Esc to cancel. Untick the box for instant changes.

Rotating a part

Use ⟳ Rotate 90° in the Parts section before placing, the ⟳ Rotate button on the confirm bar, the Rotate tool, or press R. Parts turn around their first pin.

Moving around

Scroll to zoom, hold the middle mouse button and drag to pan, and use Fit to reset. Ctrl+Z undoes. Everything snaps to the grid; change it under Board. Choose a preset from 5.08 mm down to 0.1 mm, or pick Custom… and type any size from 0.1 mm. Very fine grids hide their thin lines until you zoom in, but snapping still works.

Auto-generate: the basics

Instead of drawing by hand, you describe your circuit as a short text list. The app then places the parts on a new board and runs the copper tape between them for you. The list has two kinds of line: part lines (what you have) and connection lines (what joins to what). You type them in the box under Auto-generate on the left, then click Generate layout.

Blank lines are ignored, and so is any line that starts with #, so you can use those for notes. Notes must be on their own line. Do not put # after something else on the same line.

Part lines: NAME type

One line per part, written as a name you choose, a space, then a type from the table. The name is how you refer to the part later (for example R1, D2, U1, J1). It must be different for every part, have no spaces, and not contain a colon or a dot. Capital letters matter, so R1 and r1 are different names.

TypeWhat it isPinsHow the pins are numbered
padA single solder pad or wire point1Only pin 1.
resResistor, or any part with two legs 10.16 mm apart (such as a diode)2Pin 1 and pin 2. Either way round for a resistor.
ledLED with legs 2.54 mm apart2Pin 1 is + (the anode, the longer leg). Pin 2 is − (the cathode, by the flat side). The board shows + and − next to the pads.
h2 h3 h4 h6 h8A single row of pins at 2.54 mm spacing, such as a battery connector, switch, button or sensor2, 3, 4, 6 or 8Pin 1 is at one end, then 2, 3 and so on along the row.
dip8 dip14 dip16A chip with two rows of pins (DIP package), rows 7.62 mm apart8, 14 or 16Standard chip numbering. Pin 1 is top left (next to the notch), numbers go down the left side, then continue up the right side. On a dip8, pin 4 is bottom left, pin 5 is bottom right and pin 8 is top right.

Parts that don't match one of these shapes can still be used. Pick the type with the same number of pins and spacing, use several pad parts, or import the part from a KiCad footprint file (see below).

Connection lines: NAME: part.pin part.pin ...

Each connection line lists pins that must be joined together with tape. It starts with a label, a colon, then the pins. The label (VCC, GND, SIGNAL, anything without a colon) is only for you and for messages. Each pin is written as the part's name, a dot, then the pin number: U1.8 means pin 8 of the part named U1. Separate pins with spaces or commas. For parts that have named pins you can use the name instead of the number, for example U1.D32 or T1.OUT+. Names are not case-sensitive.

All the pins on one line end up joined together. If three things meet at one point, such as the battery +, a resistor and a chip's power pin, put all three on one line.

Rules to remember

  • Each pin can appear on only one connection line. To join more things to the same point, add them to that line instead of starting a second one.
  • A connection line needs at least two pins. A line with one pin is ignored.
  • Pins you don't list are left unconnected. The part is still placed, and tape keeps clear of it.
  • Every name used in a connection must match a part line exactly, and the pin number must exist on that part (for example R1.3 is an error because a resistor has two pins).
  • You can have up to 29 connection lines.

A worked example

# battery, resistor and LED in a loop
J1 h2
R1 res
D1 led
VCC: J1.1 R1.1
N1: R1.2 D1.1
GND: D1.2 J1.2

Read it as: J1 is a two-pin battery connector, R1 a resistor and D1 an LED. The first connection joins battery pin 1 to one end of the resistor. The second joins the other end of the resistor to the LED's + leg. The third joins the LED's − leg back to battery pin 2, completing the loop. To see a bigger example, pick one from the Load an example menu and read how it is built.

What happens when you click Generate layout

  1. The app reads your list and checks it. If something is wrong it tells you which line and stops without changing your board.
  2. It arranges the parts in rows, trying up to 40 different orders and turn angles, and keeps the arrangement where the most connections can be routed with the least tape and the smallest board.
  3. It routes tape for each connection, shortest connections first. Tape never crosses other tape, and it keeps clear of other pads.
  4. It replaces your current board with the result and sizes the board to fit. With Confirm changes on, you review it and press Confirm or Cancel (Cancel brings your old board back).

After that it's a normal board. Move parts, redraw tape, erase and rotate as usual. Click Generate again to get a different arrangement from the same list.

Settings that affect auto-generate

  • Allow 45° tape angles: lets tape run diagonally as well as straight, which usually fits more in less space and routes more connections. Untick it for straight, square corners only.
  • Tape width (left panel): auto-generate uses your chosen width, but never wider than 2 mm, so tape can fit between pins.
  • Pin hole and pad size (right panel): bigger pads take up more room, so there is less space for tape to pass between pins.

Messages you might see

  • Unknown part type: the word after the name isn't in the table above. Check the spelling.
  • Add at least one part line: the box has no part lines.
  • Net NAME: "X.n" does not match a part pin: the part name doesn't exist, or the pin number is too high or missing. The part and pin are written NAME.number.
  • Up to 29 nets are supported: you have too many connection lines. Join pins that share a point onto one line.
  • Routed N of M nets. Couldn't route: ...: those connections were left undrawn because tape had no free path. Click Generate again for a new arrangement, tick 45° angles, use thinner tape or smaller pads, or draw the missing tape by hand.

Limits

  • The board has a single layer of tape, so two lines can never cross. Very tangled circuits may not route completely. Use a short wire jumper where lines must cross.
  • You can't choose where parts go. After generating, drag them where you want and redraw tape as needed.
  • Only the part types in the table are available. Use the closest match for other components.

Importing KiCad footprints

You can add your own parts from KiCad footprint files. Click Import KiCad footprint… in the Parts section and choose one or more files. Both formats work: .kicad_mod (KiCad 5 and newer) and the older .mod (KiCad 4 and older, including library files that hold many footprints, up to 60 at a time).

  • The part appears in the Parts list under Imported (KiCad), and a message under the button tells you its type name. Type that name as the part type in Auto-generate, for example J1 screw_terminal_1x04.
  • Pins: each through-hole pad becomes a pin, in pad-number order. Use the pad number or name in connections, such as J1.1 or J1.A2.
  • Hole size: imported parts follow the Pin hole setting, the same as every other part. The drill size written in the footprint file is ignored.
  • Reference point: the lowest-numbered pad sits at the part's origin, so it lands on the grid when you place it. Other pads keep their real spacing, even if it is not on the grid.
  • Not imported: surface-mount pads, mounting holes, silkscreen, courtyards and 3D models. A message tells you how many were skipped. All pads are drawn round.
  • Imported parts are saved inside your design file, so they come back when you open it.

Check the spacing against your real component before printing, as the footprint is only as accurate as the file.

Board and export shape

Set the board size and Margin. Shrink board to fit crops the board to your design. Export shape chooses what gets exported: the whole board, a rectangle around the design, or Cut away unused, which follows the design's outline to save plastic. Your on-screen design is not changed by this.

Hole and pad size for one part

  1. Choose the Select tool (or press S) and click a part. It gets a yellow dashed outline and the Selected part box opens on the left. Clicking with the Move tool also selects.
  2. Type a Pin hole ⌀ and/or a Pad ⌀. Those sizes apply to that part only, in the picture, the STL and the SVG. Nothing else on the board changes.
  3. Leave a box empty and that part follows the Print settings. Use global sizes clears both boxes. Apply to same type copies the sizes to every part of that type.

The pad is always kept at least 0.4 mm wider than the hole so some copper ring is left. This also applies to imported KiCad parts, whose holes otherwise follow the Pin hole setting. Auto-generate routes tape with the global pad size, so set per-part sizes after generating, and check clearances if you make a pad much bigger. Per-part sizes are saved with the design.

Print settings (right)

  • Traces: Raised prints tape lines standing above the plate (Lowered makes grooves). Trace height sets how much.
  • Pin hole is the leg hole size. Pads are raised to the tape height so tape and solder have something to sit on. Tick Larger solder pads to set a bigger pad size.
  • Very thin tape lines are hard to print. Use at least 0.8 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle.

Files

  • Export STL: the printable plate. Download SVG template: a 1:1 drawing you can print on paper to check fit or cut tape against.
  • Save design / Open design: keep your work as a file and come back to it later.

Building it

  1. Print the plate. A first small test board is a good idea.
  2. Press copper tape along the raised lines, running it onto each pad. Trim neatly.
  3. Push component legs through the holes and solder them to the tape at each pad.
