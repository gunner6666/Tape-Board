How to use Tape Board

Created by R. Heller

Design a circuit board for 3D printing. You print the plate, press copper tape onto the raised lines, and solder component legs through the holes.

Quick start

  1. Choose a part such as Resistor from the Parts list (left), then click the board to place it. Parts that come in several sizes (pin headers, DIP chips, two-leg parts, SMD resistors and jumper wires) are listed once and ask which size you need; the size button under the list changes it. Press Confirm (or Enter) to keep it.
  2. Choose the Tape tool. Click where tape starts, then click where it ends. Press Confirm (Enter) to keep that piece, or Extend (E) to keep it and carry on from where it ended. Press Esc to end the run.
  3. Press Export STL (top of the Files section, right panel) and print the file.

Tools (top bar)

  • Tape (T): draw copper tape runs. Part (P): place the selected part. Select (S): click a part to pick it and give it its own hole and pad size (see below). Move (V): drag a part, a tape run or a hole. Rotate: click a part to turn it 90°. Hole (H): add a mounting screw hole. Text (L): engrave a label into the plate. Outline (O): draw a custom board shape or a cutout. Erase (X): remove a part, hole, tape run, jumper, text or outline. Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) step through your changes.

Confirming changes

With Confirm changes ticked (top bar), every change waits for approval. While it waits you can drag it to a better spot. For tape, drag the green corner dots to reshape, or drag the piece to move it. Press Enter to keep or Esc to cancel. Untick the box for instant changes.

Rotating a part

Use ⟳ Rotate in the Parts section before placing, the ⟳ Rotate button on the confirm bar, the Rotate tool, or press R. Parts turn around their first pin. Tape that ends on a pad moves with the part when you rotate, flip or drag it.

Which side do parts go on? (Flip)

Footprints are drawn as seen from the tape side. If you push a part's legs in from the plain side and solder on the tape side, chips, modules and connectors end up mirrored. Press ⇋ Flip (or F) for those parts so their pins match. Flipped parts have a dashed outline and a ⇋ after their name. Two-leg parts such as resistors don't need it. Pin 1 of every multi-pin part has a square pad so you can line chips up. The square grows to match the tape that runs onto it, so the tape never looks narrower than the pad.

Moving around

Scroll to zoom, hold the middle mouse button and drag to pan, and use Fit to reset. Ctrl+Z undoes. Everything snaps to the grid; change it under Board. Choose a preset from 5.08 mm down to 0.1 mm, or pick Custom… and type any size from 0.1 mm. Very fine grids hide their thin lines until you zoom in, but snapping still works.

Drawing a schematic (the easy way)

Instead of typing a list, you can draw your circuit and let the app build the board from it. Click ⚡ Draw schematic in the Auto-generate section (left panel). A full-screen drawing area opens.

  1. Place: pick a part from the list at the top, then tap the grid to put it down. Connectors, DIP chips and jumper wires ask which size you need when you pick them. Each part gets a name such as R1 or D1 automatically. The list uses normal schematic parts: resistor, capacitor (plain and polarised), inductor, LED, diode, battery, switch, push button, buzzer, potentiometer, NPN and PNP transistors, N-MOSFET, ground and power symbols, 2 to 8-pin connectors, chips (8, 14 and 16 pin), ESP32, rotary encoder, TP4056 module and a single pad or test point. Imported KiCad parts are listed at the end.
  2. Wire: tap one pin, then tap another pin to join them. Pins joined by wires become one connection. Every Ground symbol is joined together, and so is every Power symbol, so you can drop them wherever you need them instead of drawing long wires. Symbols such as ground are not real parts, so they do not appear on the board.
  3. Edit: tap a part to select it. A small bar appears at the bottom with the part's name, a Value box, and Rotate, Flip and Delete buttons. Drag a part to move it, and drag empty space to pan. Use − and +, the scroll wheel, or a two-finger pinch to zoom.
  4. Delete: tap a part (its wires go too) or a single wire to remove it.
  5. Values: with the Edit tool, type into the Value box to label a part — 10k, 100nF, 1N4148, and so on. The value appears under the part in orange and in the parts list. Values do not change the board; they are there so you know what to buy and fit.
  6. Rotate and flip: Rotate turns the selected part a quarter turn at a time, and Flip mirrors it, which is the quick way to swap which end a pin sits on. The symbol turns, but its name and value stay the right way up. On a keyboard, R rotates and F flips.
  7. Undo and redo: the ↶ and ↷ buttons at the top step back and forward through your changes, including placing, wiring, moving, rotating, deleting, setting a value and Clear. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y do the same.
  8. Parts list: opens a table of everything you have drawn, grouped by part and value, with the quantity and the names in each group. Copy puts it on the clipboard so you can paste it into a shopping list. Parts that usually need a value are flagged when you have not set one.
  9. Jumper wire: in the Basic group. Its two ends are one connection, like a wire in the drawing. Pick its length with Footprint.
  10. Footprint: the Edit bar also has a Footprint list showing every board footprint with the same number of pins. A plain capacitor starts on 5.08 mm legs and an electrolytic on 2.54 mm. Pick the one that matches your real part, including the SMD sizes. The drawing keeps the choice.
  11. Press Generate PCB ▸ for a fresh board, or Update PCB ▸ to keep your existing layout. Before building, the app checks the drawing over and shows anything it finds. Problems have to be fixed first: two parts sharing a name, or ground wired to power (a short circuit). Worth a look items are only warnings — parts that are not wired to anything, pins left unconnected, or missing values — and you can press Build the board ▸ to carry on. If nothing turns up, the board is built straight away.
  12. The app then writes the part and connection lines for you in the Auto-generate box, closes the drawing area and builds the board exactly as Generate layout does. Any values you set are added underneath as # notes, which the builder ignores. Use Clear to start the drawing over.

Your drawing is remembered in this browser, so it is still there next time. Ground and power connections are named GND and VCC, and the rest N1, N2 and so on. Each part is built from a matching board footprint (a resistor, capacitor or diode uses the 10.16 mm two-leg footprint, a battery or switch a 2-pin header, a transistor or potentiometer a 3-pin header numbered in the order shown on the symbol, such as B, C, E), so check your real part's leg order, and use Rotate or Flip or swap the wires if needed. Generate PCB builds a new board and replaces the current one. Update PCB keeps your layout: new parts are added below the others, deleted parts are removed, changed footprints are swapped in place, and the connections are refreshed so the board shows what still needs tape. Wires only show what joins to what. The drawing does not decide where parts or tape go on the board. You can edit the generated list in the Auto-generate box and press Generate layout again if you want to tweak it.

Auto-generate: the basics

Instead of drawing by hand, you describe your circuit as a short text list. The app then places the parts on a new board and runs the copper tape between them for you. The list has two kinds of line: part lines (what you have) and connection lines (what joins to what). You type them in the box under Auto-generate on the left, then click Generate layout.

Blank lines are ignored, and so is any line that starts with #, so you can use those for notes. Notes must be on their own line. Do not put # after something else on the same line.

Part lines: NAME type

One line per part, written as a name you choose, a space, then a type from the table. The name is how you refer to the part later (for example R1, D2, U1, J1). It must be different for every part, have no spaces, and not contain a colon or a dot. Capital letters matter, so R1 and r1 are different names.

Parts that don't match one of these shapes can still be used. Pick the type with the same number of pins and spacing, use several pad parts, or import the part from a KiCad footprint file (see below).

Connection lines: NAME: part.pin part.pin ...

Each connection line lists pins that must be joined together with tape. It starts with a label, a colon, then the pins. The label (VCC, GND, SIGNAL, anything without a colon) is only for you and for messages. Each pin is written as the part's name, a dot, then the pin number: U1.8 means pin 8 of the part named U1. Separate pins with spaces or commas. For parts that have named pins you can use the name instead of the number, for example U1.D32 or T1.OUT+. Names are not case-sensitive.

All the pins on one line end up joined together. If three things meet at one point, such as the battery +, a resistor and a chip's power pin, put all three on one line.

Rules to remember

  • Each pin can appear on only one connection line. To join more things to the same point, add them to that line instead of starting a second one.
  • A connection line needs at least two pins. A line with one pin is ignored.
  • Pins you don't list are left unconnected. The part is still placed, and tape keeps clear of it.
  • Every name used in a connection must match a part line exactly, and the pin number must exist on that part (for example R1.3 is an error because a resistor has two pins).
  • There is no limit on the number of connection lines.

A worked example

# battery, resistor and LED in a loop
J1 h2
R1 res
D1 led
VCC: J1.1 R1.1
N1: R1.2 D1.1
GND: D1.2 J1.2

Read it as: J1 is a two-pin battery connector, R1 a resistor and D1 an LED. The first connection joins battery pin 1 to one end of the resistor. The second joins the other end of the resistor to the LED's + leg. The third joins the LED's − leg back to battery pin 2, completing the loop. To see a bigger example, pick one from the Load an example menu and read how it is built.

What happens when you click Generate layout

  1. The app reads your list and checks it. If something is wrong it tells you which line and stops without changing your board.
  2. It arranges the parts in rows, trying up to 40 different orders and turn angles, and keeps the arrangement where the most connections can be routed with the least tape and the smallest board.
  3. It routes tape for each connection, shortest connections first. Tape never crosses other tape, and it keeps clear of other pads. When Use jumper wires is ticked and tape has no way round, it adds a jumper wire that hops over the tape in the way.
  4. It replaces your current board with the result and sizes the board to fit. With Confirm changes on, you review it and press Confirm or Cancel (Cancel brings your old board back).

After that it's a normal board. Move parts, redraw tape, erase and rotate as usual. Click Generate again to get a different arrangement from the same list.

Settings that affect auto-generate

  • Allow 45° tape angles: lets tape run diagonally as well as straight, which usually fits more in less space and routes more connections. Untick it for straight, square corners only.
  • Tape width (left panel): auto-generate uses your chosen width, but never wider than 2 mm, so tape can fit between pins.
  • Pin hole and pad size (right panel): bigger pads take up more room, so there is less space for tape to pass between pins.

Messages you might see

  • Unknown part type: the word after the name isn't in the table above. Check the spelling.
  • Add at least one part line: the box has no part lines.
  • Net NAME: "X.n" does not match a part pin: the part name doesn't exist, or the pin number is too high or missing. The part and pin are written NAME.number.
  • X.n is in both A and B: one pin is listed on two connection lines. Merge them into one line.
  • Routed N of M nets. Couldn't route: ...: those connections were left undrawn because tape had no free path. They show as dashed yellow lines. Click Generate again for a new arrangement, tick 45° angles or jumpers, use thinner tape or smaller pads, or draw the missing tape by hand.

Update board (keep layout) reads the same list but does not move anything already placed. It adds new parts below the others, removes parts no longer listed, changes footprints, and loads the new connections.

Limits

  • The board has a single layer of tape, so two lines can never cross. Where they must, the router adds a jumper wire, or you can place one from the Jumper wires group in the Parts list.
  • You can't choose where parts go. After generating, drag them where you want and redraw tape as needed.
  • Only the part types in the table are available. Use the closest match for other components.

Importing KiCad footprints

You can add your own parts from KiCad footprint files. Click Import KiCad footprint… in the Parts section and choose one or more files. Both formats work: .kicad_mod (KiCad 5 and newer) and the older .mod (KiCad 4 and older, including library files that hold many footprints, up to 60 at a time).

  • The part appears in the Parts list under Imported (KiCad), and a message under the button tells you its type name. Type that name as the part type in Auto-generate, for example J1 screw_terminal_1x04.
  • Pins: each through-hole pad becomes a pin, in pad-number order. Use the pad number or name in connections, such as J1.1 or J1.A2.
  • Hole size: imported parts follow the Pin hole setting, the same as every other part. The drill size written in the footprint file is ignored.
  • Reference point: the lowest-numbered pad sits at the part's origin, so it lands on the grid when you place it. Other pads keep their real spacing, even if it is not on the grid.
  • Surface-mount pads are imported as rectangles with no hole. Through-hole pads are drawn round, with pin 1 square.
  • Not imported: mounting holes, silkscreen, courtyards and 3D models. A message tells you how many were skipped.
  • Imported parts are saved inside your design file, so they come back when you open it.

Check the spacing against your real component before printing, as the footprint is only as accurate as the file.

Connections on the board (dashed lines)

When the board comes from the schematic or the Auto-generate list, it remembers which pins belong together. Any connection that has no tape yet is shown as a dashed yellow line between the nearest pads, just like KiCad's ratsnest. Draw tape along it and the line disappears. Hover over a pad to see its part, pin and connection name in the bottom-left corner. The Design summary shows how many connections are still to route and warns about short circuits as you work. Parts you place by hand get names automatically (R1, D1 and so on), and you can rename them with the Select tool so they match your list.

Checking the design

Press Check design (right panel) before printing. It finds short circuits between different connections, copper closer than Min spacing, tape narrower than Min tape width, tape that stops short of a pad, connections not yet routed, parts that overlap, copper off the board or in a cutout, mounting holes cutting into copper, and thin copper rings around holes. Problems are circled red and warnings orange on the board; tap an item in the list to zoom to it. The circles clear when you change the design.

Jumper wires

The quickest way is the Jumper wires group in the Parts list: pick a length, place it like any part and rotate it to point where you need. Its two ends count as connected. The blue line shows an insulated wire that passes over the board. When Use jumper wires is ticked, Auto-generate also adds jumpers wherever tape has to cross; those can be any length or angle, and you can move or erase them like any other item.

Engraved text and labels

Choose Text (L), click the board and type your label. It is cut 0.3 mm into the plate, so it prints cleanly. Size it with Text size in Print settings, turn it with the Rotate tool, and drag it with Move. Engrave part names and LED + adds every part's name and a + by each LED's long leg automatically. Part names and + marks find their own clear spot next to each part, away from tape and pads, and move if you redraw tape. If a part is boxed in, the name shrinks a little or moves further out, and if there is truly no room it is left off and Check design tells you which one. Your own text stays where you put it; Check design warns if it ends up under tape.

Board shape and cutouts

Set Corner radius for rounded corners. For any other shape, set Outline tool draws to Board shape, choose Outline (O) and click the corners; click the first corner again, double-click or press Enter to close it. Switch to A cutout to draw holes or notches in the plate. Remove custom outline goes back to a rectangle.

3D preview and tape cut list

3D preview shows exactly what the STL will be: drag to turn it and zoom with − and +. Tape cut list lists every tape run with its connection, width, length and number of bends, plus jumper wire lengths, so you can cut tape before you start. Copy puts the list on the clipboard.

Board and export shape

Set the board size and Margin. Shrink board to fit crops the board to your design. Export shape chooses what gets exported: the whole board, a rectangle around the design, or Cut away unused, which follows the design's outline to save plastic. Your on-screen design is not changed by this. A custom outline always exports as drawn.

Hole and pad size for one part

  1. Choose the Select tool (or press S) and click a part. It gets a yellow dashed outline and the Selected part box opens on the left, where you can also change its Name. Clicking with the Move tool also selects.
  2. Type a Pin hole ⌀ and/or a Pad ⌀. Those sizes apply to that part only, in the picture, the STL and the SVG. Nothing else on the board changes.
  3. Leave a box empty and that part follows the Print settings. Use global sizes clears both boxes. Apply to same type copies the sizes to every part of that type.

The pad is always kept at least 0.4 mm wider than the hole so some copper ring is left. This also applies to imported KiCad parts, whose holes otherwise follow the Pin hole setting. Auto-generate routes tape with the global pad size, so set per-part sizes after generating, and check clearances if you make a pad much bigger. Per-part sizes are saved with the design.

Print settings (right)

  • Traces: Raised prints tape lines standing above the plate (Lowered makes grooves). Trace height sets how much.
  • Pin hole is the leg hole size. Pads are raised to the tape height so tape and solder have something to sit on. Tick Larger solder pads to set a bigger pad size.
  • Hole compensation is added to every hole in the STL, because printers make holes a little small. 0.2 mm suits most printers; measure a test print and adjust. The SVG template keeps the true size.
  • Very thin tape lines are hard to print. Use at least 0.8 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle.

Files

  • Export STL: the printable plate. Download SVG template: a 1:1 drawing you can print on paper to check fit or cut tape against.
  • Save design / Open design: keep your work as a file and come back to it later. The file holds the board, its connections, imported parts and your schematic drawing.

Building it

  1. Print the plate. A first small test board is a good idea.
  2. Press copper tape along the raised lines, running it onto each pad. Trim neatly.
  3. Push component legs through the holes and solder them to the tape at each pad.
